# UESolDS source provenance and label validity — 2026-11-12

**Finding:** source restriction alone does not create a reliable binary solubility cohort. Original records are recoverable, but the audited structural-genomics negatives do not document measured insolubility. DNASU offers a more promising explicit-outcome route, with unresolved construct mapping.

This audit concerns the frozen 13,880 development proteins only. Source names below are the existing header-derived proxies; programme-level facts do not prove per-record origin, host or assay. Earlier reports and original labels remain unchanged.

## Published construction versus original measurements

The [UESolDS paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11343611/) assigns TargetTrack negatives from early workflow stages (tested, selected, cloned, expression tested), and positives from expression/solubility/purification/structure progress. Work-stopped/other records were reportedly excluded. DNASU uses explicit Protein_Soluble versus Tested_Not_Soluble fields. eSOL is described as score-present versus score-absent; PDB E. coli-expression entries become positives. These are published construction rules, not recovered assay outcomes. Upstream filtering also removes negative sequences similar to positives, which can alter biological associations.

Missing expression, failed crystallization, unattempted expression and experimentally insoluble protein are different outcomes. Even successful expression does not quantify its soluble fraction. None should be silently recoded.

The [eSOL archive](https://dbarchive.biosciencedbc.jp/en/esol/data-1.html) reports 4,132 entries, of which 3,173 have solubility measurements, with distinct chaperone conditions. It measures soluble fraction after centrifugation in a cell-free system. This contradicts interpreting every missing value as measured insolubility; it does not identify which released UESolDS records originated there.

Programme selection can intentionally enrich families, organisms, lengths or tractable constructs. Such biological sampling is a valid explanation of sequence/source prediction, not automatically a laboratory artefact. The [PSI production-centre account](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0969212607004753) describes allocation of protein families between centres and sequence-based target selection. Thus centre and family are structurally associated upstream of any solubility assay.

## Direct TargetTrack recovery: fixed 24-record sample

The [original TargetTrack archive](https://zenodo.org/records/821654) was downloaded outside the repository. Its published MD5 was verified (`200012a8a2a11ffd7e370ed142df36c3`). We scanned the complete XML and saved only the selected records and referenced protocols. The archive documents target sequences separately from trial sequences; a target can have multiple modified experimental constructs.

Sampling was prespecified: JCSG/MCSG × training/validation × label, with shortest, median-length and longest proteins in each cell (24 total, deterministic raw-ID tie breaks). This deliberately covers lengths and labels; **it is not a random prevalence estimate**. No record was selected by probe score.

- **24/24 IDs and exact target sequences recovered.** Exact trial sequences were recovered for 8/12 JCSG and 12/12 MCSG records. Four JCSG records differ from all available trial sequences.
- **12 negative-labelled records:** nine have exact trial matches, whose histories contain only selection/cloning. The other three have matching target sequences but different trial constructs, also at early workflow stages. No explicit negative solubility measurement was recovered for any of the twelve. This does not prove those proteins are soluble; it means the negative label is not justified by a recovered measurement.
- **12 positive-labelled records:** eleven have exact trial matches; five reach purification, including two MCSG records with an explicit soluble stage. Six show expression without a soluble stage; one JCSG target lacks an exact trial sequence. These evidence levels must remain distinct.
- Multiple trials occur for the same exact sequence. One MCSG positive target has both stopped and successful trials. A single target-level maximum status discards condition-specific failures and successes.
- Four sampled JCSG targets have current target status “work stopped,” despite their inclusion in the release. Trial statuses differ from aggregate target status. The released status field, snapshot and upstream selection implementation cannot be reconstructed just from the published description.

Evidence: [sample definition](source_audit_results/recovery_sample.tsv), [target matches](source_audit_results/targettrack_sample_matches.tsv), [all sampled trials](source_audit_results/targettrack_sample_trials.tsv), [XML snippets and protocols](source_audit_results/targettrack_sample/), [archive checksums](source_audit_results/targettrack_archive.json). All statuses are preserved literally; no missing measurement became a negative.

## Assay and construct comparability

The recovered archive contains 4 JCSG, 7 MCSG, 85 SECSG and 4 SGPP expression-protocol definitions (some text is missing). These are programme-wide definitions, not counts of protocols verified for this cohort; see [protocol counts](source_audit_results/targettrack_protocol_counts.tsv). A centre name cannot collapse these definitions into one assay.

Recovered JCSG protocols distinguish expression from purification and describe changes in induction procedures over time. Recovered MCSG protocols include overnight 18°C induction and preparation of a soluble-fraction sample; other referenced expression protocol text is absent. Neither archive provides a uniform quantitative soluble-fraction result for the sampled negatives. The [MCSG vector study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2792030/) directly demonstrates that fusion constructs can change solubility screening outcomes. This supports treating construct identity as part of the measurement, not stripping tags to “clean” the outcome.
The published UESolDS E. coli-expression restriction does not make growth strain, vector, induction temperature, lysis, salt, detergent, cofactors, fusion partner or assay threshold identical. Same centre is therefore not same assay. Protein species must come from independent sequence/reference mapping, not the expression host.

## DNASU recovery pilot

A separate fixed sample covers three length-ranked records per split × label (12; six per label). All twelve clone pages were recovered and all twelve explicit soluble-status fields agree with the released labels. This is **stronger outcome evidence than a selected/cloned status**. However, none exactly matches the listed translated insert: eleven released sequences equal the listed insert plus C-terminal LE; the remaining pET15 record differs differently. The repository describes vector fusions, so an insert match would still not certify the assayed full fusion sequence.
The [sample table](source_audit_results/dnasu_sample_matches.tsv) records statuses, vector, source, hashes and exact prefix/suffix differences. [Raw public pages](source_audit_results/dnasu_raw_pages.zip) preserve the retrieval. An initial feasibility lookup of clone 627866 is archived too but excluded from the prespecified twelve-sample counts. No tags were stripped and no labels were changed.
This pilot supports prioritizing DNASU for recovery; it cannot certify all 460 DNASU-proxy records or establish one comparable assay. DNASU is a repository: its sampled pages name NESG as the contributing source, illustrating overlap between a repository proxy and a laboratory centre.

## Smallest practical resolution step

Recover the original assayed construct sequence, vector/tag layout, expression/solubility score and protocol/version for the six sampled DNASU negatives and six positives. Obtain the pre-cleaning sequence-to-clone crosswalk from UESolDS authors or the NESG/DNASU original assay records, and reconcile the terminal changes without modifying the frozen cohort. If this succeeds, audit all 51 DNASU negatives and the associated positive records (460 proteins total) under the same rule. This has clearer expected value than extracting more embeddings or choosing the best-performing centre.
For JCSG/MCSG, a negative requires a recorded soluble-expression failure after detectable expression or a quantitative soluble-fraction result, with an exact assayed construct and protocol. Selection/cloning histories cannot supply that evidence. A new assay or a centre LIMS export may be necessary. No external messages were sent.
