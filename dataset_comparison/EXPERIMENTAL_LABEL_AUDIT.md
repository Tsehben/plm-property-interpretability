# Experimental label audit — 12 November 2026

**The best-supported binary outcome is NESG high expression with extreme soluble recovery. PSI:Biology provides much broader binary-label support, but less recoverable quantitative assay information. Neither measures universal, condition-independent protein solubility.** All counts here are computed from the downloaded files, not copied from paper abstracts.

## Original experimental definitions

Price et al. describe overnight expression at 18°C in E. coli BL21λ(DE3), pET constructs with short His tags and pMGK-supported rare tRNAs. At least two clones informed small-scale gel assessment. E describes expression; S describes the soluble fraction after centrifugation: S = 0 means no soluble recovery, S = 5 approximately complete recovery. E/S are **ordinal scores**, not precise continuous measurements. The study states that S was not recorded when E = 0. Selection excluded many membrane/low-complexity targets and included isolated domains; this limits biological scope. The original analysis removed tags computationally. These are study-level statements, not record-by-record protocol verification for every entry in the later mirror. [Price et al., 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3372292/).

DNASU stores centre-supplied expression, solubility and purification outcomes separately in a controlled vocabulary. `Protein_Soluble` and `Tested_Not_Soluble` therefore convey explicit outcomes, unlike absence of structural-genomics progress; `Not_Tested` and `Tested_Not_Found` must not be converted into insolubility. Controlled vocabulary alone does not establish identical assays across centres or vectors. [Original PSI Material Repository description](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkp999).

Bhandari et al. used 8,780 pET21 and 3,436 pET15 proteins expressed in E. coli. Their available cleanup code excludes class 0, invalid nucleotide strings and internal translation stops before mapping class 1 to insoluble and class 2 to soluble. We reproduce those exact totals and retain the translated tagged construct alongside the unmodified released NetSolP sequence. [SoDoPe paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7750957/), [pinned original cleanup code](https://github.com/Gardner-BinfLab/SoDoPE_paper_2020/blob/c28f26d94aa6285ff08270709fc8a95806ddb1db/Data_cleanup.ipynb).

NetSolP specifies soluble E = 4–5/S = 4–5 and insoluble E = 4–5/S = 0; its usability objective uses E×S > 11 versus < 4. It reports 25% global-identity partitioning and additional PSI-vs-NESG filtering. Those steps explain why score filtering alone does not produce its 1,323-record external set. [NetSolP methods](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/264717113/btab801.pdf), [official datasets](https://services.healthtech.dtu.dk/services/NetSolP-1.0/).

## Findings from the actual source files

- The raw NESG archive has 9,703 records / 9,478 distinct sequences. There are 206 repeated-sequence groups comprising 431 records. Of those, 150 groups / 316 records disagree in E or S. The remaining 56 groups have consistent scores. Keeping one deterministic record from each consistent group yields 9,328 sequences; dropping every repeated group yields 9,272. Neither procedure erases the original evidence.
- The 59 E = 0 / S > 0 entries happen numerically to equal the difference between 9,703 and 9,644. **This is not proof that these were the additional records.** No record is removed merely to recover a published total.
- A numeric S in an E = 0 archive row is not accepted as a measured negative. Strict and sensitivity solubility policies require positive, integer E. All nine fractional E records remain flagged, unrounded and outside our derived integer-score policies.
- In the released NetSolP NESG file, MAR120 has E = 4.5, S = 0; MAR123 has E = 3.5, S = 5; PFR1 has E = 3.5, S = 0. The released labels are preserved. Removing only these integer-rule exceptions gives 841 soluble / 479 insoluble records.
- The pET15 raw export allows more direct outcome verification than pET21. The previous DNASU recovery pilot is reused from `../Violet969-PLM_Sol-3589a95/curation/source_audit_results/dnasu_sample_matches.tsv`, including its construct mismatch caveats. It is a small selected pilot, not proof for all 12,216 records.
- No gel images, full replicate-level outcomes or complete per-record assay-condition table were recovered. The original Price supplementary DOC was retrieved and inspected; it contains supplementary analyses and methods rather than the missing full sequence/score membership table. The initial PMC download returned HTML; that failed response is explicitly identified in `source_manifest.json` and is not treated as data.

## Consequences

High-expression NESG negatives have a substantially clearer interpretation than workflow-derived UESolDS negatives. PSI binary labels are also a meaningful improvement, but sequence-label conflicts and vector-dependent constructs need explicit flags. Differences between PSI and NESG can reflect different constructs, experimental updates, score thresholds or assay conditions; identical-sequence disagreement is preserved as evidence, not automatically deemed a labeling error.

Do not concatenate these releases as independent cohorts. Do not treat high E as an innocuous covariate filter: conditioning on expression changes the target population and may affect biochemical associations. Retain the full score-bearing archive for descriptive/ordinal analyses, with primary binary readouts restricted by a declared outcome rule.
